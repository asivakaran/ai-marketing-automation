# SALES KIT — selling AutoPilot Marketing OS
### Written for a founder starting with £0, no case studies, no office, no car.

Everything in this kit costs nothing to execute. The product runs free (mock AI, mock SMS logged to console, live email alerts via a free Gmail App Password). Your first sale funds the paid upgrades.

---

## 1. What you are actually selling

Not software. **Missed revenue.** A roofer spends £500–£2,000/month on ads. Studies across the trades consistently show ~70% of website visitors leave without contacting anyone — and speed-to-lead decides who wins the job (a lead contacted within 5 minutes is ~10x more likely to convert than one contacted an hour later).

Your one-liner:

> "Your website lets people leave. I install an assistant that talks to them, captures their details, and texts you within seconds. You only ever see it when there's money on the table."

The buyer never sees code. They see: **a chat bubble on their site → their phone buzzing with a hot lead → a dashboard showing £ pipeline.** That's the product.

## 2. The pricing ladder (do not skip rungs)

| Rung | When | Price | What you say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pilot** | Weeks 1–2 | **FREE, 14 days** | "No charge, no card. If it doesn't capture leads, you've lost nothing." |
| **Founding client** | Your first **3** clients only | **£0 setup + £99/mo, cancel anytime** | "You're one of my first three, so the setup — normally £1,500 — is free. All it costs is £99/month, and a quick testimonial if it's worked for you." |
| **Standard** | After 3 case studies with numbers | **£750–£1,500 setup + £99/mo** | "That's less than one reroof. One captured lead pays for it." |
| **Case-study rate** | After 6 case studies | **£2,500–£3,500 setup + £149/mo** | Show the numbers: "Captured 11 leads → 4 booked jobs → £19K work." |
| **Premium** | 10+ case studies, niche authority | **£5,000 setup + £199/mo** | You now have proof nobody can argue with. |

**Why is the founding rung nearly free?** Because your first 3 clients are worth more as **proof** than as fees. A stranger will not gamble £1,500 on an unknown — but £99/month with no setup and no lock-in is a decision a roofer can make in 30 seconds. Each founding client gives you a case study, a testimonial, and a reference call — the three assets that make the £1,500+ rungs real instead of a bluff. Tradesmen smell bluffs; this ladder never bluffs.

Monthly fees matter more than setup fees anyway. 10 customers at £99/mo = **£990/month recurring** — that's the business. The setups are bonuses; the recurring is the salary.

## 3. Who to target (and who to skip)

**Target:** trades running paid ads (Facebook/Google) — they already pay for leads, so they already believe in buying leads. Check their website: if there's no chat, they're leaking.

Best niches in order: **roofers > plumbers > electricians > landscapers > builders.** High job value (£2K–£15K per job) means even ONE captured lead pays for your fee.

**Skip (for now):** sole traders with no website, big firms with in-house marketing, anyone who "wants to think about it" twice — that's a no, move on.

**Finding them:** Facebook groups for UK trades, Google Maps (search "roofer High Wycombe" — anyone with a website and no chat bubble), your parents' friends who run businesses, local Facebook marketplace sponsorships.

## 4. Outreach — right channel, right message

**Where trades actually are (in order):**

1. **SMS / WhatsApp to their BUSINESS number** — the one printed on their website and Google listing. That's their public line and where their attention lives. This is your primary channel.
2. **In person (highest conversion):** trade counter mornings — Screwfix, Jewson, Travis Perkins, builders' merchants — 6:30–8am. Job-site walk-ups work too. **A parent comes. Always.** In person, your age becomes an asset: nobody forgets the kid who built an AI system.
3. **Phone — but only as follow-up,** never cold. After a text, offer: "Want me to ring you Tuesday around 11 for 10 minutes?" Quiet hours for trades: 10–12 and 2–3:30. A booked call with a warm prospect beats 20 cold calls.
4. **Facebook — groups, not DMs.** Cold DMs to strangers die in Message Requests. Instead: be present in local trade/community groups, answer questions, become a name. Message business pages only if they're visibly active.

**The message (works as text/WhatsApp/in-person opener):**

> Hi [Name], I came across [Business] while looking at [their trade] in [town]. Quick one — I noticed your website doesn't have a way to catch people who visit but don't call. I've built a system that chats to them 24/7, grabs their details, and texts you within seconds when there's a hot lead. I'm setting it up for a couple of local trades free for 2 weeks to get real results I can show. No charge, no card details. Worth 10 minutes to see it on your own site?

Send 10 a day. Expect 1–2 replies. That's normal and fine — 10/day = 70/week = enough. No reply in 4 days = ONE polite bump, then move on.

## 5. The demo script (the moment that sells it)

Do this IN PERSON or on a video call, phone in hand. Have the demo app open (login: `demo@apexroofing.com` / `demo1234`) and `test-widget-demo.html` on your phone.

**Power move — make the demo about THEM:** before the call, take 5 minutes to set up a demo tenant in THEIR business name and THEIR brand colour (tenants carry their own name + brand colour). When the chat bubble pops up saying "[Their Business] — chat with us", the demo stops being "some software" and becomes "my company, working." This one trick does more for the close than any pitch line.

1. **Show the widget** (30 sec): "This sits on your website. Customer taps it, asks anything — 11pm, Sunday, whenever."
2. **Be the customer** (60 sec): Open the demo host page, type: *"My roof is leaking badly in the storm, water's coming through the ceiling — how fast can someone come out? HP13."* — give it your real mobile number when it asks.
3. **The close moment**: their phone/email gets the alert while you're still sat there. Say nothing. Let it buzz. Wait 3 seconds. Then: "That's a customer you'd have lost. It's 11pm. You were asleep."
4. **Show the dashboard** (30 sec): Overview screen, point at the pipeline number FIRST — "This screen turns chats into money. Booked jobs times your average job value. Tune it to your real prices in Settings."
5. **Ask for the pilot**: "Two weeks free on your actual website. If it doesn't capture a single lead, you've lost nothing and I'll take it off. If it works, we talk numbers. Fair?"

If they hesitate: "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?" — then listen. Their answer is the deal.

## 6. Handling the hard questions

**"How old are you?" / "You're 13…"**
> "I built this. It's running right now on your screen. The age on my passport doesn't change whether your phone buzzes when there's a £4,000 job on the table. The grown-up who signs the paperwork is [parent/guardian] — the product works either way."

Let the demo answer. Every second they spend arguing with your age is a second they're not disputing the product. Also: a 13-year-old who built this is *more* impressive, not less. Own it.

**"We already have a website."**
> "A website is a billboard. This is a salesperson who never sleeps. Your website gets visitors who leave — this catches them before they go to [competitor]."

**"What if it breaks / who fixes it?"**
> "It's monitored, and I fix issues same-week — you have my direct number, not a ticket queue. That's actually the advantage of hiring me over an agency."

**"Too expensive."** (only ever at paid rungs)
> "One reroof is £5–10K. If this catches one job all year it's paid for itself several times over. And you're not paying for software — you're paying for the leads it catches at 11pm that you never see."

**"Can you just build me a website instead?"**
> "I can — but a website alone doesn't capture anyone at 11pm. This is the piece that makes your existing site produce."

## 7. The pilot agreement (keep it to one page)

Free 14-day pilot terms — write them plainly, get a signature (parent co-signs):

1. **Free for 14 days.** No card. No obligation to continue.
2. **What you get:** chat assistant on your website, instant lead alerts (email + SMS), lead dashboard access.
3. **What I get:** permission to publish the results ("[Business] captured X leads in 14 days") — anonymised numbers if you prefer.
4. **Data:** lead details belong to the business. I remove access at the end if you don't continue.
5. **If you continue:** setup fee + monthly price agreed IN WRITING before the pilot ends (tell them the price BEFORE the pilot starts — never spring it after; that's how you build trust at rung 2).

**Trial-end conversation (founding-client close):** "In 14 days you captured X leads, Y booked jobs — worth roughly £Z of work. You're one of my first three clients, so the setup — normally £1,500 — is free. It's £99/month to keep it running, cancel anytime, and if it's worked for you I'd want a quick testimonial. Shall I leave it on?"

## 9. The math to keep you honest

- 10 outreach texts/day → 2–4 conversations → 1 pilot/week
- 4 pilots → 2 convert as founding clients at £99/mo (£0 setup)
- **Month 1–2 goal:** 2–3 founding clients (£198–£297/mo recurring) + the case-study screenshots — the text that says "that thing just booked me a job" is worth more than any single setup fee
- **Month 3–6:** 3+ case studies → switch to £750–£1,500 setup rung
- **Month 6+:** 10 customers ≈ £990–£1,490/mo recurring + setup fees on every new client

Recurring is the prize. Every month you keep customers happy is a month you get paid for work you did once.
